Purpose of the Down Payment

A down payment is an upfront lump-sum payment made when purchasing a home — typically a percentage of the purchase price (commonly 20%). It serves several key purposes: it reduces the loan amount (mortgage principal) the buyer must borrow, which lowers monthly payments and total interest paid over the life of the loan. It also demonstrates financial commitment to the lender, reducing their risk, which can lead to better interest rates. Additionally, putting down 20% typically allows the buyer to avoid Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI), an extra monthly cost lenders require when the loan-to-value ratio is too high.
